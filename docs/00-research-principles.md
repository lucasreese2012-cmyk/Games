# 00 — Research: Environmental Design Principles

This is not a review of 22 games. It is a list of the mechanisms that make their landscapes work, pulled apart so they can be rebuilt with southeastern American materials. Each principle names the games that show it most clearly, explains *why* it works on a person moving through a landscape, and states how the Cassena Islands use it. Nothing here is copied: no layouts, no place types that only make sense in those settings.

---

## 1. Geographic design

### 1.1 Barriers decide everything downstream
**Seen in:** Red Dead Redemption 2 (the Grizzlies wall off the north; the Lannahechee and Kamassa split the east), Skyrim (the Throat of the World and the mountain ring around the provinces), Death Stranding (ridgelines and rivers that are physically hard to cross), Kingdom Come: Deliverance (the Sázava valley and forested hills that funnel traffic to fords and towns).

**Mechanism.** A believable world has a small number of large barriers and everything else is a consequence of them: roads go round, towns sit at the gaps, rivers run along them. When barriers are placed first, the rest of the map explains itself. When attractions are placed first and barriers added to separate them, the geography reads as stage scenery.

**Applied.** Four barriers were placed before anything else: the Balsam Crest (a 2,047 m crystalline range on Halcomb), the Blue Wall (a south-facing escarpment on Graystone), Cassena Sound (a rift trough 14 m deep that separates the highlands from the coastal plain) and the Ossahatchee swamp (impassable except along one relict beach ridge). Every highway, rail line and town in the islands is a response to one of these four. I-21 exists where it does because Shady Gap is the only low saddle through the crest's western spurs; Tarrow Landing exists because Long Ridge is the only dry ground reaching Tarrow Sound.

### 1.2 Water is the skeleton
**Seen in:** Kingdom Come (mills, villages and roads strung along the river), The Witcher 3 (Velen's marsh-and-river network; Skellige's fjords), Ghost of Tsushima (rivers and coast define provinces), Far Cry 5 (Hope County's river valleys and lakes), Assassin's Creed Origins (the Nile as the spine of the whole map).

**Mechanism.** Watersheds give a landscape a hidden logic the player feels before they understand it: valleys open downstream, towns get bigger toward the mouth, bridges cluster where water is narrow. Following water is the oldest way of navigating and the most natural for a player.

**Applied.** Thirty-eight named rivers were carved with continuous downhill beds and checked by an automated pass (every river falls to the sea or a lake). Settlement size grows downstream on every watershed: Upper Cove (250) → Calloway (900) → Tennalee Falls (24,000) → Calder (362,000) on the Tennalee. The fall line, where crystalline highland rivers drop onto the coastal plain, is a real belt of towns, mills and bridges along the south foot of Halcomb.

### 1.3 Elevation is a reward
**Seen in:** Elden Ring (Liurnia viewed from the Stormveil cliffs; vistas over a whole region), Ghost of Tsushima (the climb to shrines on peaks), Skyrim (High Hrothgar's long climb), Assassin's Creed series (synchronisation points), Metro Exodus (the Volga's water tower and crane views).

**Mechanism.** Height turns the map into a picture. A climb is effort; the view at the top pays for it by showing the next three destinations at once. The best worlds tie viewpoints to physically reasonable high ground rather than arbitrary towers.

**Applied.** Viewpoints are on real high points, fire towers, bridge crests and observation decks, and every one was tested with a line-of-sight model that includes earth curvature, refraction and forest canopy. The Ledford Dome Tower exists because the archipelago's highest summit is otherwise buried in spruce–fir; from it 64 landmarks, summits and towns are visible.

### 1.4 Coastlines need process, not outline
**Seen in:** Assassin's Creed IV: Black Flag (reefs, keys, mangroves, sandbars at sailing scale), Far Cry 3 (volcanic islands with fringing reefs and lagoons), Far Cry 6 (Caribbean coasts of very different types), The Witcher 3 (Skellige's rocky fjord coast).

**Mechanism.** A coast is believable when its shape tells you what made it: sand barriers are long and straight with inlets; drowned valleys are branching; rocky coasts are cliffed with coves; mangrove coasts are lobed and blurred. Mixing processes randomly produces the "pie-crust" coast typical of procedural worlds.

**Applied.** Every island's coast was generated with a signed-distance outline and noise tuned to its process: barrier islands have almost no low-frequency wobble and break only at inlets; sea islands have fractal marsh edges; Halcomb has a rough mountain coast; Calder has a nearly straight built-out seawall. Barrier islands face the open ocean; marsh fills the lagoons behind them; inlets sit where tidal exchange is forced.

### 1.5 Valleys and passes as composition
**Seen in:** Red Dead Redemption 2 (Cumberland Forest's passes; the Heartlands opening out from Valentine), Days Gone (the canyon highways of Belknap and Cascade), Ghost of Tsushima (golden forests framing mountain passes), Death Stranding (valleys as routes, ridges as obstacles).

**Mechanism.** A pass is a natural frame: the road narrows, climbs, and opens onto a new landscape. A long valley produces depth in every screenshot because the eye follows the floor toward the far end.

**Applied.** Ledford Gap, Sawyer Gap and Shady Gap are real low points on the Balsam Crest, chosen by examining the generated ridgeline, and each pass opens onto a different world (Ledford Gap: from coastal foothills to the cliffed North Face; Shady Gap: from limestone valley to tidal coast). The Coldwater Valley is a long limestone trough floored with farms between the crest and a sandstone tableland: a 20-km "picture" with a river, a railway and an interstate laid along it.

### 1.6 Wetlands and forests are terrain, not fill
**Seen in:** The Witcher 3 (Crookback Bog), Red Dead Redemption 2 (Bluewater Marsh and Lagras), STALKER (forest belts and swamps around Chernobyl), Fallout 4 (Far Harbor's fog-bound bog), Kingdom Come (managed forests between villages).

**Mechanism.** In a good world, vegetation and wetness answer to the ground. Marshes are flat and low. Hardwood fills the sheltered coves. Pines take the dry ridges. When this holds, the player reads drainage and soil without being told.

**Applied.** Thirty-three land-cover classes are assigned by rules on elevation, slope, aspect, curvature, distance to water, soil proxy and latitude: cove hardwood in concave north-facing hollows, oak–hickory on convex south-facing ridges, spruce–fir only above about 1,850 m, longleaf on deep sand, cypress in permanently flooded flats, salt marsh below 1.7 m in sheltered water, mangrove only on the southernmost keys. The register then names fifty specific stands and paints them on terrain the rules already agree with.

### 1.7 Caves, cliffs and the vertical edge
**Seen in:** Elden Ring (sheer cliffs that hide whole regions below), Fallout: New Vegas (canyon walls of the Mojave), Metro Exodus (Taiga cliffs and the Caspian's eroded scarps), Far Cry 3.

**Mechanism.** A cliff is a one-way window: you see what lies below but cannot simply walk there, so you look for the way down. Caves and rock shelters reward close inspection of these edges.

**Applied.** Cliffs are limited to where geology allows them: the Callahan North Face (a fault scarp falling to The Narrows), the Blue Wall escarpment, the Fallon Cliffs on Graystone's sea coast, the Tableland Bluffs (sandstone caprock) and the walls of Hollow Reach. Rock shelters sit under the sandstone caprock (Hickory Falls Rock Shelter), sinks and springs in the Wickham limestone. There are no caves on the coastal plain where there is no rock to hold them.

## 2. Exploration design

### 2.1 The landmark you see before you can reach it
**Seen in:** Skyrim (the Throat of the World visible from everywhere), Elden Ring (the Erdtree), Assassin's Creed Odyssey (statues and temples on headlands), GTA V (the Vinewood sign, Mount Chiliad, the Maze Bank tower), Cyberpunk 2077 (Arasaka Tower and the megabuildings).

**Mechanism.** A distant, unique silhouette gives orientation and a goal at once. It works best when there are only a few, they differ in shape, and they belong to their setting.

**Applied.** Twenty-two primary landmarks, each with a distinct silhouette: a 518 m TV mast, a pair of hyperbolic cooling towers, a spiral-ramp summit tower, a suspension bridge across a fjord-like gorge, a 64-storey tower with a lit spire, a 60 m checkerboard-painted lighthouse, an offshore wind farm. Their visibility was measured: the TV mast can be seen from 42% of all land in the islands, the summit tower from 35%.

### 2.2 Breadcrumbs: a chain of reasons to keep going
**Seen in:** Breath of the Wild-style design as absorbed by Elden Ring and Ghost of Tsushima (golden birds, wind, smoke columns), The Witcher 3 (signposts and roadside shrines), Red Dead Redemption 2 (campfire smoke, birds, railway telegraph poles).

**Mechanism.** The player never needs a marker if each place shows the next. A smoke column, a power line, a creek, a rail line or a water tower all point somewhere.

**Applied.** Linear human features are used as breadcrumbs in the way they work in reality: transmission lines run from the Corliss stacks and Ocosta towers to substations across the islands; abandoned rail grades (a cypress tram, a turpentine tram, an excursion line) lead from towns into the wilds; fire towers stand on the few hills of the flat islands; boardwalks and cable inclines lead off roads into forest.

### 2.3 Alternate routes, shortcuts and chokepoints
**Seen in:** Red Dead Redemption 2 (old roads and trails parallel to the main routes), Days Gone (forest roads that bypass highways), Kingdom Come (field tracks between villages), Death Stranding (the player cuts their own routes).

**Mechanism.** A world feels real when there is more than one way between two points and the choice has consequences: shorter but steeper, longer but easier, faster but exposed.

**Applied.** Every major journey has at least two routes with different character. Calder to Coldwater: I-21 through Shady Gap (49 km, 33 min) or the Balsam Crest Parkway along the crest (slow, high and scenic). Halcomb to Graystone: the Gate Road over the Gate Bridge or the Graystone–Calder ferry. The swamp is crossed only by the Trail on Long Ridge — a deliberate chokepoint, because nothing else could be built there.

### 2.4 Layered landscapes and high/low traversal
**Seen in:** Cyberpunk 2077 (vertical city: street, overpass, megabuilding), Assassin's Creed Valhalla and Odyssey (cliff paths above shorelines), Metro Exodus (river valley with railway, village, and ridge), GTA V (Los Santos freeway stacks and the hills behind).

**Mechanism.** Several routes stacked in the same view — a river at the bottom, a road on the valley side, a railway higher up, a ridge path on top — make even a small area feel deep.

**Applied.** The Lower Tennalee Gorge stacks the river, the main line railway, SR 28 and the 230 kV line from the Calloway powerhouse. The Coldwater Valley stacks river, interstate, main line and the old valley road. In Calder the passenger branch runs on the upper deck of the Market Street lift bridge above the river traffic.

### 2.5 Micro-discoveries and unexpected vistas
**Seen in:** Red Dead Redemption 2 (strange small details off the road), Fallout 3 and 4 (skeletal scenes and small rooms), STALKER (anomalies of the ordinary: a rusted bus, a drained pool), Elden Ring (a sudden lake beyond a cave).

**Mechanism.** Small, specific, physical oddities make a world feel inhabited beyond what the player is shown. The best are unexplained but legible: a rope swing over a spring, a chimney in a dry lake bed, a car in a creek.

**Applied.** Twenty-four micro-landmarks — a rope swing over the Blue Hole, a line of old car bodies laid along an eroding creek bank, a catfaced turpentine pine, a freeze-killed mangrove stand at the northern limit of mangroves, a survey marker on a sand hill — are placed where the physical processes that produced them actually operate. None carries an explanation beyond the physical one.

### 2.6 Environmental contrast at walking distance
**Seen in:** The Witcher 3 (Toussaint's vineyards against the Velen mire), Metro Exodus (Volga swamp, Caspian desert, Taiga forest), Far Cry 6 (city, farmland and jungle within a few kilometres), Ghost of Tsushima (each province's colour signature).

**Mechanism.** Contrast is what makes each environment visible. A marsh is more vivid after a mountain; a city more dense after farmland.

**Applied.** The islands were arranged so that the full sequence from spruce–fir to mangrove occurs along a single 95 km drive, and several abrupt transitions exist within 2–5 km: the Calder skyline 2 km across water from the cooling stacks of Corliss; the North Face rising 1,900 m above the cold tidal Narrows; Ocosta's nuclear towers rising behind longleaf sandhills.

## 3. Atmospheric design

### 3.1 Weather has geography
**Seen in:** Red Dead Redemption 2 (fog in Lemoyne's bayous, snow in the Grizzlies, dust in New Austin), Ghost of Tsushima (wind as a constant presence), The Witcher 3 (Skellige storms), Death Stranding (timefall belts on the mountains).

**Mechanism.** When weather is tied to terrain — fog in valleys at dawn, storms building over mountains in the afternoon, clear air after a front — it becomes part of the place rather than a global filter.

**Applied.** Each region in the [atmospheric pass](16-atmospheric-pass.md) has its own weather drivers: sea-breeze thunderstorms over the flat southern islands in summer afternoons; orographic cloud on the Blue Wall's south face (the wettest place in the archipelago); radiation fog in the Coldwater Valley and on the Tennalee; sea fog in The Narrows when warm air crosses cold water; rime ice on the Balsam Crest in winter.

### 3.2 Light as material
**Seen in:** Ghost of Tsushima (golden grass at low sun), Red Dead Redemption 2 (volumetric light through forest), Cyberpunk 2077 (neon and wet streets), Assassin's Creed Origins (desert glare and dusk).

**Mechanism.** Low, warm light across a textured surface — grass, water, marsh — is more beautiful than any object. A landscape designed with known sun directions produces repeatable moments.

**Applied.** Viewpoints carry a facing direction; west-facing coasts (Mirabel, Wickham) were given the sunset beaches, east-facing barrier islands (the Gannet Banks, Ambrose Beach) the sunrise beaches, and the Calder skyline is lit from the south-west in the evening when seen from the Tennalee River Bridge.

### 3.3 Sound is a landmark
**Seen in:** STALKER (Geiger clicks, distant howls), Red Dead Redemption 2 (frogs in the bayou, wind on the plains), Kingdom Come (bells across valleys), Days Gone (silence and wind on the roads).

**Mechanism.** A signature sound fixes place more strongly than a texture: surf, spring peepers, a mill whistle, a rail horn in a valley.

**Applied.** Each region has a signature sound in the atmospheric pass (surf on the Banks, cicadas in the sandhills, airboats on the Trail, the Corliss paper-mill whistle, the rattle of the lift bridge bells). A paper-mill whistle is even listed as a micro-landmark.

### 3.4 Visibility is a design tool
**Seen in:** STALKER and Metro Exodus (fog and haze to limit and reveal), Fallout 3 (the washed-out capital wasteland horizon), Death Stranding (distance haze shows scale).

**Mechanism.** How far you can see changes what a landscape means. A clear winter day reveals connections between regions; summer haze isolates each one.

**Applied.** Visibility tests use a 60 km haze limit, the realistic best case for winter cold-air days in the humid Southeast. In summer, haze closes to 10–20 km and the islands become separate worlds again.

## 4. Environmental storytelling (physical evidence only)

### 4.1 Infrastructure layers show time without words
**Seen in:** Fallout 3, Fallout 4, Fallout: New Vegas (the abandoned and the repurposed side by side), Metro Exodus (railway decay), Days Gone (collapsed bridges, rusting vehicles), STALKER.

**Mechanism.** Several generations of infrastructure on the same route — an old ferry slip below a modern bridge, a swing bridge next to a high-rise, a narrow-gauge grade beside a highway — let the player infer the order of things without any explanation.

**Applied.** Abandoned features sit beside their successors: the Old Narrows Ferry Slip under the Narrows Bridge; the collapsed truss of Old Hominy Bridge lying in the creek beside its concrete successor; the Ambrose Beach Line trestle pilings beside the Bonnet Inlet Bridge; an unfinished railway incline (Blue Wall Incline) with tunnel portals that open on nothing. Nothing in the registers says why they were abandoned.

### 4.2 Altered landscapes
**Seen in:** Fallout 4 (quarries and craters), Kingdom Come (charcoal clearings, stone quarries), Red Dead Redemption 2 (strip mines, logging camps), Far Cry 5 (silos and dams).

**Mechanism.** Human alteration of the ground itself — pits, spoil heaps, reservoirs, cut-overs, canals — reads as authentic because it follows resource geology.

**Applied.** Quarries are on hard rock (Coldwater limestone, Cutstone granite); kaolin pits on the Bellamy fall-line sands; phosphate pits on the Wickham karst; dredge-spoil mounds and a landfill hill on Corliss; a drowned valley with a mill chimney under Calloway Lake; rice-field impoundments with timber trunk gates on Saint Ambrose; a straight canal (the Sill Canal) through the swamp.

### 4.3 Reclamation by vegetation
**Seen in:** The Last of Us and Days Gone (roads swallowed), Red Dead Redemption 2 (ruins under ivy), STALKER, Metro Exodus.

**Mechanism.** The speed and type of regrowth tells the player about climate: kudzu over a house in Georgia-like humidity is unmistakable.

**Applied.** Kudzu House on Graystone's Piedmont strip, the Hemlock Ghost Forest (dead grey hemlocks), boneyard beaches of dead live oaks where the shoreline has retreated through the maritime forest, and houses standing in the surf at the eroding end of Ambrose Beach.

## 5. Realism of the built environment

### 5.1 Road hierarchy
**Seen in:** GTA V (freeways, boulevards, county roads, dirt tracks), Red Dead Redemption 2 (a clear hierarchy of wagon roads and trails), Kingdom Come, Days Gone.

**Mechanism.** A believable road system has a few big roads, many medium ones and countless small ones, each built to a grade and curvature standard matching its traffic. Players read the hierarchy instantly.

**Applied.** Seven classes, each with an enforced maximum grade (interstate 5%, highway 6%, state 8.5%, parkway 8%, arterial 10%, county 13%, railway 2.2%). Every route was laid out by a grade-constrained search across the terrain, then given a vertical alignment with cut, fill, bridges, viaducts and tunnels. I-21 crosses Halcomb without a single tunnel because Shady Gap is low enough; the Gate Road needs the 1.65 km Bearpen Tunnel and a 249 m-high suspension bridge because nothing lower exists there.

### 5.2 Towns where towns would be
**Seen in:** Kingdom Come (villages at fords and on hill-shoulders), Red Dead Redemption 2 (railroad towns, river ports), GTA V (Los Santos sprawling along the coast plain and freeways).

**Mechanism.** Every settlement in a real region has an answer to "why here?" — a crossing, a harbour, a junction, a resource, a gap. Size follows the strength of that reason.

**Applied.** Fifty-one settlements, each with a geographic reason recorded in its entry (head of navigation, fall-line crossing, railroad junction on flat ground, ferry slip at the narrowest crossing, dry island in a swamp). The biggest city sits on a bedrock island at the mouth of the largest river beside the deepest water in the Sound — the strongest combination of reasons in the archipelago.

### 5.3 Industry, power and utilities that connect
**Seen in:** Fallout 4 (power networks), Days Gone (substations and water towers), Far Cry 5 (grain elevators and silos), GTA V (port, refinery and power plant clusters).

**Mechanism.** Utilities give a landscape its connective tissue. Power lines run from plants to substations; water towers stand on high ground in towns; industry clusters where rail, deep water and labour meet.

**Applied.** Five plants (gas, nuclear, hydro, solar, offshore wind), ten substations and the transmission lines between them were checked for connectivity. Corliss, the industrial island, has 15.5 m of water at its container berths, the freight railway, the port interstate spur and the power station; Ocosta Nuclear sits beside the Ocosta estuary and Ketch Sound, which supply its cooling water, and its lines run north to the Bellamy substation.

### 5.4 Airports and ports obey physics
**Seen in:** GTA V (an airport on reclaimed flat land by the sea), Far Cry 5 (rural airstrips on valley floors), Assassin's Creed IV (harbours in sheltered deep water).

**Mechanism.** Runways need long flat ground and clear approaches; ports need deep sheltered water. Getting this wrong is visible from the air.

**Applied.** Eight runways were graded within 1% longitudinal slope, and every 50:1 approach surface was tested against terrain and trees (all clear, after clearing runway protection zones). Fourteen harbours were checked against measured water depth at the berth.

## 6. One transferable lesson per game

| Game | The single principle carried into Cassena |
|---|---|
| Red Dead Redemption 2 | Regions defined by ecology and weather, joined by roads that feel built for wagons, trucks and freight rather than for the camera. |
| GTA V | Density from a real urban hierarchy: core, mid-rise ring, industrial port, freeway suburbs, then rural edges within a short drive. |
| Ghost of Tsushima | A strong colour signature for each region so it can be recognised from one frame. |
| Cyberpunk 2077 | Verticality in a dense core — stacked transport and towers on a small island. |
| Skyrim | One or two summits that dominate every horizon and act as a compass. |
| The Witcher 3 | Distinct regional identities built from geology, vegetation and architecture together. |
| Assassin's Creed IV: Black Flag | A sea-scale archipelago where the water itself is varied (reefs, flats, channels, sounds). |
| Assassin's Creed Origins | A river as the organising spine of settlement. |
| Assassin's Creed Odyssey | Landmarks on headlands and peaks, read from the sea. |
| Assassin's Creed Valhalla | Fjord-like drowned valleys and cliff routes above them. |
| Far Cry 3 | Island coasts that change type around one island (reef, cliff, mangrove, beach). |
| Far Cry 5 | Rural American infrastructure (silos, water towers, county roads) as landmarks. |
| Far Cry 6 | Subtropical city, farmland and wild coast within a short distance. |
| Days Gone | Road-based exploration of wild country: the road itself as the experience. |
| Death Stranding | Terrain that makes the route the puzzle: slope, rivers and rock decide the way. |
| Elden Ring | Hidden lowlands seen first from a cliff edge. |
| Kingdom Come: Deliverance | Settlement logic of fords, mills and valley farms at real-world scale. |
| STALKER | Atmosphere from ordinary industrial decay and silence. |
| Fallout 3 | The horizon as a ruined-infrastructure skyline. |
| Fallout: New Vegas | Long sightlines across dry ground to distant landmarks. |
| Fallout 4 | Coastal fog and bog regions with a distinct palette from the inland. |
| Metro Exodus | A journey through successive environments, each changing gradually into the next. |

These lessons are applied through the physical design only. The islands carry no story, factions or narrative; everything a visitor learns, they learn from the ground.
