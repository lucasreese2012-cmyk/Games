# 17 — Exploration Psychology

Why people want to look round the next corner, and how the Cassena Islands' physical design gives them reasons to — without markers or instructions. Every example below is a real line of sight or route on the generated map.

## 1. Layered visibility

A satisfying view has four planes: **foreground → middle distance → distant landmark → horizon**. The islands are arranged to produce them constantly.

| From | Foreground | Middle distance | Distant landmark | Horizon |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ledford Gap Overlook (Halcomb) | Stone wall and the Parkway's curve | The Ledford Prong valley falling away in spurs | The WCDR mast 37 km south and the Ocosta cooling towers at 52 km | The flat southern islands fading into haze |
| Tennalee River Bridge crest | The bridge's concrete parapet | Calder's skyline rising from the water | The Ledford Dome tower on the crest, 17 km away | The Balsam Crest, ridge behind ridge |
| Grayback summit (Graystone) | Bare granite with weathering pits | Piedmont farms and the Bright Water Gullies | The Blue Wall's waterfalls | The Corliss stacks, Calder's towers and, on clear days, the WCDR mast 53 km away |
| Iron Mountain (Bellamy) | Ironstone ledges and cedars | Fields, bays and pine woods | The WCDR mast, the Ocosta cooling towers | The mountains of Halcomb, 30 km north |
| Kestrel Hill (Gannet Banks) | Bare sand ripples | Kestrel village and Tarrow Sound | Cape Merrin Lighthouse, the wind farm | The Atlantic on one side; Graystone's distant domes on the other |
| Big Water Tower (Ossahatchee) | Boardwalk rail | Open black water, cypress and the Skyway's long line | The Highpine Fire Tower 19 km east and the Ocosta cooling towers 20 km north | Cypress domes to the edge of the world |

Measured: the Calder Tower deck sees 66 landmarks, summits and towns; the Ledford Dome tower 64; even Landfill Hill on flat Corliss sees 60, out to 60 km, because it stands alone above a flat island in the middle of the Sound.

## 2. Curiosity gaps

Roads that disappear make people want to follow them. The terrain does this without contrivance:

- **Behind hills:** every southern spur of the Balsam Crest ends in a knob; SR 8 and I-21 swing around them, and the next cove is hidden until the bend.
- **Behind forests:** the shell roads of Ambrose Main vanish into oak tunnels; the Wickham tram grade fades into longleaf.
- **Behind buildings:** Calder's grid hides the harbour until the last block, then opens onto ships.
- **Behind cliffs:** the Narrows Road ends where the North Face cliffs begin — the cliffs continue round the corner toward Big Laurel, and the road does not.
- **Around bends:** the Highlands Road on Graystone drops into three gorges; each descent hides what is on the other side.
- **Over bridges:** the Gate Road leaves the Bearpen Tunnel onto a curving viaduct, and the Gate Bridge appears only at the last curve. The Ambrose Inlet Bridge's hump hides the Banks until its crest.
- **Into the water:** old roadbeds run straight into Calloway Lake and reappear at drawdown; the Cypress Tram Grade runs into the swamp and ends.

## 3. Landmark chaining

One landmark should reveal another. The analysis computes what each primary landmark can see of the others ([15 — Realism Audit](15-realism-audit.md)). Several designed chains follow:

1. **The crest chain:** Leaving I-21 at Ledford, the Gap Road climbs and the **Ledford Dome Tower** appears above the trees → reaching it reveals **Calloway Lake** in its bowl, the **Callahan North Face** dropping to The Narrows, and the **Narrows Bridge** → from the bridge the **Lantern Mountain radar dome** on Graystone is visible to the east.
2. **The Sound chain:** From the **Tennalee River Bridge**, the **Corliss Stacks** stand east across the harbour → from the stacks (or Landfill Hill beside them) the **Cape Merrin Lighthouse** and the **Merrin Shoals wind farm** appear far to the south-east, nearly 60 km away → from Cape Merrin, **Kestrel Hill**, the big dune, is visible up the Banks.
3. **The farm chain:** Crossing the Long Bridge, the **WCDR mast** is the tallest thing ahead → from the mast's fields, the **Ocosta cooling towers** rise to the south → beyond them lies the swamp, with only the **Highpine Fire Tower** on Long Ridge breaking the line.
4. **The escarpment chain:** From Graystone town, **Grayback**'s bare dome stands in front of the Blue Wall → from Grayback's summit, **Thunderhole Falls** shows as a white stripe in a slot → the Whitlock Grade climbs beside it to **Lake Whitlock** and the highland.
5. **The coast chain:** From the end of **Mirabel Pier** the white beach runs south toward the foreland and **Cape Serena Light** → from the light, the **Scrub Hill Fire Tower** stands over the trees 15 km across the pass on Wickham → from the fire tower, the **Sabal Bridges** and **Cayo Lejos Light** lie on the flats 32–38 km to the south-east.

## 4. Environmental contrast

Dramatically different environments are close together wherever the geology allows:

| Transition | Distance | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Spruce–fir summit → salt water | 6 km horizontal, 2,000 m vertical | Mount Callahan to The Narrows |
| Mountain forest → farmland | 2 km | The crest's western spurs to the Coldwater Valley floor |
| High-rise city → heavy industry | 2 km across the harbour | Calder Point to Corliss |
| City → farmland | 5 km | The Long Bridge's south end to Kinard's fields |
| Farmland → tidal marsh | 1 km | Bellamy's south coast |
| Longleaf savanna → nuclear station | Adjacent | Ocosta |
| Highland lake → rainforest escarpment → red-clay Piedmont | 4 km | Lake Whitlock to Bright Water village |
| Swamp → barrier beach → open ocean | 8 km | Long Ridge across Tarrow Sound to the Banks |
| Resort towers → wild foreland | 3 km | Condo Row to the Cape Serena dune lakes |

## 5. Natural discovery

Places worth finding are placed where the physical processes that create them operate, so curious people find them by reading the land rather than following a marker:

- **Follow water downhill** on Halcomb's tableland and you reach Hickory Falls and the rock shelter behind it.
- **Follow a creek uphill** on the North Face and you find cascades, then old-growth coves.
- **Walk the foot of a sand ridge** on Wickham and you find springs where the limestone surfaces.
- **Look at the shape of a lake** on Bellamy: if it is oval and has a white sand rim on its south-east side, it is a bay with a beach.
- **Follow a straight line** in a curving landscape — a power line, a tram grade, a canal — and it leads to something human.
- **Look for a gap in a cliff line** and there is a cove with a beach and a landing (Fallon, Piney Cove, Big Laurel).
- **Watch for change in the trees**: a ring of cypress in a pine flatwood is a pond; a line of cypress is a stream; a dark dome on a summit is spruce.

None of this requires instruction. It is how the real landscape works, and the islands were built so that it works here too.
