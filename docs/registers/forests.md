## Halcomb Island

### Ledford Spruce–Fir Forest

- **Island:** Halcomb Island
- **Location:** 8.6 km SSE of Pigeonroost (grid 29.6 E / 80.4 N)
- **Forest type:** Spruce–fir
- **Extent:** about 11.3 km²
- **Description:** The archipelago's only boreal forest: dense red spruce and Fraser fir above about 1,850 m, floored with moss, wood sorrel and blowdowns, with grey dead fir on the exposed shoulders.
- **Geographic justification:** The summits are cold enough — mean annual temperature near 7 °C — to support a relict northern forest left from colder climates.
- **Visual identity:** Almost black conifer canopy, often in cloud; the trees drip even on dry days.
- **Nearby features:** Ledford Dome, Mount Sawyer, Coldspring Knob, the Parkway.
- **Why it is memorable:** Walking out of a southern hardwood forest into what smells like Canada.

### Balsam Crest Northern Hardwoods

- **Island:** Halcomb Island
- **Location:** 11.7 km E of Coldwater (grid 24.0 E / 78.6 N)
- **Forest type:** Northern hardwoods
- **Extent:** about 22.6 km²
- **Description:** A belt of yellow birch, American beech and yellow buckeye along the high crest, twisted by ice storms, with beech gaps of short, gnarled trees in the saddles.
- **Geographic justification:** The cool band between the oak forests and the spruce–fir.
- **Visual identity:** Gold in mid-October, earlier than the valleys.
- **Nearby features:** Painted Bald, Stormhead Mountain, the Parkway.
- **Why it is memorable:** Moss-draped birch trunks in fog.

### Callahan Old-Growth Coves

- **Island:** Halcomb Island
- **Location:** 5.5 km WSW of Big Laurel (grid 32.0 E / 85.6 N)
- **Forest type:** Cove hardwood and rhododendron
- **Extent:** about 11.1 km²
- **Description:** Uncut cove forest on the North Face: tulip poplars and buckeyes two metres across, a jungle of rhododendron along the streams, and a ground layer of trillium and fern.
- **Geographic justification:** The North Face was too steep for the logging railroads; deep, moist coves facing north grow the largest trees.
- **Visual identity:** Enormous straight grey trunks rising into a closed canopy.
- **Nearby features:** Mount Callahan, Callahan Branch, Big Laurel Creek.
- **Why it is memorable:** Trees so wide three people cannot reach around them.

### Big Laurel Second Growth

- **Island:** Halcomb Island
- **Location:** 2.7 km S of Big Laurel (grid 37.4 E / 84.6 N)
- **Forest type:** Cove hardwood and rhododendron
- **Extent:** about 12.1 km²
- **Description:** Even-aged second-growth forest over the old logging grade, with rusting cable, rail spikes and a collapsed trestle in the hollows.
- **Geographic justification:** Logged by railroad a century ago, now grown back as dense young forest.
- **Visual identity:** Straight, crowded trees all the same size.
- **Nearby features:** Big Laurel Incline, Big Laurel Top, Big Laurel Millpond.
- **Why it is memorable:** Iron artefacts of the logging era scattered in the leaf litter.

### Hemlock Ghost Forest

- **Island:** Halcomb Island
- **Location:** 5.6 km SSW of Big Laurel (grid 36.0 E / 81.8 N)
- **Forest type:** Cove hardwood and rhododendron
- **Extent:** about 2.2 km²
- **Description:** A stand of dead eastern hemlocks, grey and needle-less, standing over a thicket of rhododendron and young hardwoods.
- **Geographic justification:** Cool, north-facing coves where hemlock once dominated.
- **Visual identity:** A grey patch on the green mountainside.
- **Nearby features:** Hemlock Knob, Big Laurel Creek headwaters.
- **Why it is memorable:** Silent grey trunks above green undergrowth.

## Graystone Island

### Blue Wall Rainforest

- **Island:** Graystone Island
- **Location:** 10.8 km N of Graystone (grid 64.0 E / 79.0 N)
- **Forest type:** Cove hardwood and rhododendron
- **Extent:** about 36.8 km²
- **Description:** A temperate rainforest on the escarpment face: hemlock, rhododendron, ferns, mosses and liverworts on every surface, with waterfalls in every ravine.
- **Geographic justification:** Moist sea air rises up the escarpment and drops over 2,500 mm of rain a year — the wettest place in the islands.
- **Visual identity:** Saturated green; the escarpment seems to steam after rain.
- **Nearby features:** Glassface Dome, Thunderhole Gorge, the Whitlock Grade.
- **Why it is memorable:** Moss so thick the rocks look upholstered.

## Bellamy Island

- **Island:** Bellamy Island
- **Location:** 2.3 km NNE of Ocosta (grid 21.0 E / 34.0 N)
- **Forest type:** Bottomland hardwoods
- **Extent:** about 15.7 km²
