## Environmental progression

The archipelago is one ecological system laid out as a staircase from the mountains to the subtropical sea. The main road south shows all of it in about two hours:

1. **High mountain forest** — Ledford Dome, 2,047 m. Spruce–fir in cloud, rime in winter. Northern hardwoods and grass balds on the crest just below.
2. **Cove forest and foothills** — The Gap Road falls through cove hardwoods and rhododendron to the motels of Ledford and the fall line.
3. **Fall line** — Tennalee Falls, where the river drops off crystalline rock onto coastal sediment. Brick mills, the first bridges, the head of navigation.
4. **The city on the rift** — Across the North Channel on the Tennalee River Bridge into Calder, a granite island of towers in the Sound, with the port and power station of Corliss across the harbour.
5. **Upper coastal plain** — Over the Long Bridge onto the Bellamy Bluffs and into farmland: red clay, cotton and peanuts, longleaf on the Red Hills, oval bay lakes, the TV mast and the cooling towers.
6. **Lower coastal plain and tidewater** — East across the Ashwood River into Saint Ambrose: live oaks and moss on the sea-island cores, then marsh to every horizon and the tide in the creeks.
7. **Swamp** — Over the Ketch Sound Bridge onto Long Ridge, with the cypress swamp of Ossahatchee spreading west to the horizon and The Trail running straight into it.
8. **Barrier coast** — East across Tarrow Sound to the Gannet Banks' dunes, inlets and lighthouse on the open Atlantic; west the other way, the Gulf's white sand on Mirabel.
9. **Subtropical keys** — South along Long Ridge to Sabal Landing and across the low bridges to the mangrove keys, the warmest and flattest land in the islands.

Side branches show the variations: the **Coldwater Valley** and **Hickory Tableland** (limestone farms and a sandstone plateau west of the crest); the **Blue Wall** and the **Graystone Highlands** (a granite escarpment of waterfalls above a red-clay Piedmont, the wettest place in the islands); **Wickham** (dry sandhill longleaf and blue springs on limestone, nearly uninhabited).
