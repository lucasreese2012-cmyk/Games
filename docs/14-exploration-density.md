# 14 — Exploration Density

The aim is a world that never goes visually flat for long and never crowds itself. Two failure modes are avoided: an hour of identical pine forest, and a landmark every thirty seconds. The spacing below is set by the physical environment — what is plausible in each kind of country — and then measured on the generated map.

## Spacing rules

| Element | Target spacing | How it is achieved |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary landmarks** | At least one visible on the horizon from most open ground | 22 primaries, chosen for height and silhouette (mast, towers, stacks, summits, bridges, lighthouse). Measured: one is visible from two-thirds of all land, including from inside forest where canopy blocks most views. |
| **Secondary landmarks** | One coming into view every 5–10 km of travel | Waterfalls, fire towers, lighthouses, quarries, bridges, water towers placed where the processes that make them operate. |
| **Local landmarks** | Every 2–3 km in settled or worked country | Ruins, old bridges, fish camps, barns, wrecks — along roads, rivers and coasts. |
| **Micro-discoveries** | Every few hundred metres on foot in rich country | A rope swing, a scarred pine, a survey post, a car bank: small physical details off the road. |
| **Scenic viewpoints** | One every 10–20 minutes of driving, more on mountain roads | 50 viewpoints on real high ground, decks, bridges and towers. |
| **Settlements** | Towns every 15–25 km on highways; villages every 5–8 km in farm country; hamlets at every crossing, landing and gap | Each settlement has a geographic reason (see the island profiles), so their spacing follows the land: dense on the fertile and coastal ground, absent in the swamp interior and on the crest. |
| **Roads** | Major roads every 5–10 km in farm country, one per valley in the mountains, one through the swamp | Roads go where grades and ground allow, then fill in with county and dirt roads. |
| **Trails** | A trail network wherever roads cannot go — the crest, the North Face, the Blue Wall rim, the swamp | Footpaths, boardwalks, canoe and airboat trails, old tram grades. |
| **Waterways** | A creek or river crossing every 1–3 km in the highlands; slow rivers every 5–10 km on the plain | The drainage density follows rainfall and rock: dense on wet, impermeable highland rock, sparse on permeable sand and limestone. |
| **Natural formations** | One distinctive formation per landscape type within a 20–30 minute walk | Domes, balds, sinks, springs, cliffs, dunes, shell rings. |

## Breathing room

Some places are deliberately sparse, because the real landscape would be — and because emptiness makes the next discovery feel earned:

- **The Ossahatchee interior**: 25 km of cypress and prairie with one road across it. The spacing between places is the widest in the islands (about 2 km); the experience is the swamp itself.
- **Wickham's ridge**: open longleaf where the next thing is a fire tower on the horizon.
- **The Callahan North Face and Hollow Reach**: no roads at all; seen from the water or from the crest.
- **The high Graystone plateau**: one winding road and miles of forest between lakes.

## Rich ground

Other places are dense because people and processes concentrate there: Calder (58 registered places per 100 km²), the Gannet Banks (62 per 100 km² on a thin strip of sand), Mirabel (33), the Tennalee Falls–Ledford foothills, the Coldwater Valley.

## Reading the numbers

- On the **highland islands** a new registered place comes up every 5–8 km of road (5–7 minutes), with constant change of view between them: the roads are on mountainsides, crossing streams every few hundred metres.
- In **Calder, Mirabel and the Banks**, the gap falls to under 2 km — a built or beach environment where something is always in view.
- On **Bellamy and Saint Ambrose**, 5–6 km between places, with farmsteads, crossroads and marsh views between.
- In the **swamp and on Wickham**, 8 km — the long empty straights of The Trail, broken by the Skyway, the airboat landing, the fish camp.
- Everywhere, the register is a floor rather than a ceiling: farm buildings, bridges, creeks and the island-specific details in each profile's exploration section fill the gaps without needing a name.
